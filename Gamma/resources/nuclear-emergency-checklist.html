Family Planning

Nuclear Emergency
Family Checklist

A plan made in advance is the difference between an orderly response and a panicked one. This step-by-step checklist covers shelter preparation, supply lists, communication plans, medication protocols, and what to do if family members are in different locations when an alert is issued.

Nuclear emergency preparedness is not about fear — it is about having a plan that exists before you need it. The families that do best in any emergency are the ones who made decisions in advance: where to shelter, who to contact, what supplies they have, and what to do if the plan changes. This checklist is sourced to the FEMA Ready.gov nuclear preparedness framework, CDC emergency radiation guidance, and FDA potassium iodide protocols. You may also want to review our shelter-in-place and 7-10 rule guide alongside this checklist — understanding how long to stay inside and why is as important as having the supplies to do it. Work through it when nothing is happening. That is when it actually helps.

01

Identify Your Best Shelter Location

Not all shelter is equal. In a radiological event, the goal is to put as much mass as possible between you and the outside environment. Concrete and brick walls, soil, and water all absorb gamma radiation. The center of a multistory building on the middle floors — away from exterior walls, roof, and ground floor — offers the most protection in most structures.

  • Identify the best room in your home for shelter — interior rooms on middle floors of multistory buildings; basement with concrete walls if available.
  • Identify shelter at your workplace — know in advance which area would offer the most protection.
  • Identify shelter for each school your children attend — know the school's shelter-in-place policy and where they will take students.
  • Know whether you live in a Nuclear Power Plant Emergency Planning Zone — check the NRC's reactor location list for the nearest facility.
02

Build Your Shelter-in-Place Supply Kit

FEMA recommends a minimum 72-hour supply kit for any emergency. For a radiological scenario, a two-week supply is a more realistic planning horizon given the timeline for fallout to decay to safer levels. Supplies should be stored in your identified shelter location.

  • Water — one gallon per person per day for at least 14 days. Store in sealed containers away from light.
  • Food — non-perishable items requiring no refrigeration or cooking (or minimal cooking). A 14-day supply per person.
  • Battery-powered or hand-crank NOAA weather radio — the most reliable alert channel when cell networks are overloaded. Extra batteries.
  • Flashlights — with extra batteries. At least one per person.
  • First aid kit — fully stocked, including prescription medications for all household members (30-day supply minimum).
  • Potassium iodide (KI) tablets — 130mg for adults, 65mg for children. Store per FDA guidance. Only take on official direction. See our full KI guide for dosing details.
  • Plastic sheeting and duct tape — for sealing windows, doors, and ventilation openings to reduce outside air infiltration.
  • N95 masks or respirators — reduce inhalation of particulates if you need to briefly go outside.
  • Change of clothes and sturdy shoes — sealed in plastic bags. Used for decontamination protocol if needed.
  • Important documents — copies of IDs, insurance, medical records, and financial documents in a waterproof container.
  • Cash in small bills — ATMs and card readers may be offline.
  • Manual can opener — if any canned food in your supply.
03

Build Your Family Communication Plan

In an emergency, cell networks become congested quickly. Text messages move through congested networks better than voice calls. Have a plan that does not depend on real-time phone contact working perfectly.

  • Designate an out-of-area contact — someone outside your region that all family members can text to relay their status. Local calls may fail while long-distance gets through.
  • Write down key phone numbers — do not rely on contacts stored only in phones. A written list survives a dead battery.
  • Establish a household meeting point — for scenarios where family members can safely move. Also a backup meeting point if the primary is inaccessible.
  • Know each child's school emergency policy — schools will shelter children in place. Know whether you should go to the school or wait for official all-clear instructions before attempting to retrieve them.
  • Text first, call second — agree on this protocol so everyone knows how to communicate when networks are stressed.
  • Consider a two-way radio or GMRS radio for household communication if cellular fails entirely. A GMRS handheld set works without cell infrastructure.
04

Seal Your Shelter Space

When sheltering in place from a radiological event, reducing outside air infiltration significantly reduces your exposure to airborne particles. This does not require permanent construction — plastic sheeting and duct tape applied quickly to windows, doors, and ventilation openings is effective and fast.

  • Pre-cut plastic sheeting to fit each window and door in your shelter room. Label and store rolled with tape so setup takes under 10 minutes.
  • Turn off HVAC systems — heating, cooling, and ventilation fans draw outside air in. Turn off all air handling during a shelter-in-place order.
  • Close and seal the fireplace damper if applicable.
  • Identify exterior vents that feed into your shelter room — seal these with plastic sheeting and tape when the order is issued.
05

Know Your Information Sources

During a nuclear emergency, information quality matters as much as any physical preparation. Know in advance which sources to trust and which to ignore.

  • NOAA Weather Radio — broadcasts Emergency Alert System messages. Works when internet and cell networks do not.
  • Local emergency management agency — know your county or city's emergency management website and social media accounts now.
  • FEMA Ready.gov — ready.gov/nuclear-explosion has official guidance updated in real time.
  • NuclearThreatWatch.org live map — use to monitor regional ambient gamma levels for situational awareness. If you have a personal Geiger counter, our home monitoring guide explains how to use it alongside network data. Follow official instructions for all decisions.
  • Do not evacuate based on social media alone — unverified reports spread faster than accurate ones in emergencies. Wait for official guidance before moving.

Safety Tip

Review this checklist with your household at least once a year. Phone numbers change, medications change, children's schools change, and family situations evolve. An emergency plan is only useful if it reflects reality. Set a calendar reminder — same day every year — to review supplies, update contact lists, and walk through the plan with everyone in the household including children old enough to understand it.

When Alerted

Immediate Actions

1. Get inside the sturdiest building available immediately.

2. Move to the center of the building, away from windows.

3. Turn off all HVAC and ventilation.

4. Tune to NOAA radio or official emergency broadcasts.

5. Do not leave until officially told it is safe.

KI Reminder

Potassium Iodide

Only take KI on the direction of public health officials. It protects the thyroid only — not the whole body. Wrong timing reduces its effectiveness.
