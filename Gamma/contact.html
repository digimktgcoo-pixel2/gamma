Emergency Communications — When the Grid Goes Down

NuclearThreatWatch.org keeps you informed via the internet — but in a major nuclear or infrastructure emergency, cell towers fail, internet goes down, and your smartphone becomes a paperweight. The single most overlooked piece of nuclear preparedness gear isn't a Geiger counter or a stockpile of food. It's a way to communicate with the people around you when every digital channel is gone.

Two-way radios are your answer. They require no cell network, no internet, no infrastructure at all — just charged batteries or a hand crank. In a localized emergency, they let you reach neighbors, family members in different parts of your home or property, and anyone else nearby who has a radio on the same channel. More importantly, millions of Americans already own two-way radios — which means in a neighborhood emergency, there's a real chance someone nearby can hear you even if you've never met.

We recommend starting with a quality GMRS or FRS two-way radio over a ham radio for most families. Ham radios cover far greater distances and are invaluable for serious preppers — but they require an FCC license to transmit legally and have a steeper learning curve. Most people in your immediate area won't have one. Two-way radios on shared FRS/GMRS channels give you immediate neighborhood reach with zero setup and no license required. That said — it never hurts to have both. Ham radio extends your range dramatically when you need to reach someone miles away.

Safety Tip

Store your radios charged and on the same channel as your household members right now. Decide on a primary channel and a backup channel with your family today — before an emergency. Write the channel numbers on a piece of tape on the back of each radio. In a grid-down scenario, you won't have time to figure out how to program them. Two minutes of prep today is worth an hour of confusion later.
